# Global Discovery Strategy

The registry should search for psychotherapy and counseling dialogue across languages and regions rather than relying on English-language search terms.

## Discovery principle

Search in layers: English research terminology; local-language equivalents; country and regional terminology; therapy-modality terminology; corpus/data/repository terminology; publication and citation trails; and dataset-hosting platforms.

A result is a **discovery candidate** until its metadata and access conditions are verified.

## Search dimensions

For each target language or region, combine terms for psychotherapy, counseling, therapy session, therapeutic dialogue, clinical conversation, psychological support, counseling transcript, psychotherapy corpus, dialogue dataset, clinical dialogue corpus, and conversational mental-health data.

Then combine those terms with country, language, institution, modality, population, and repository names.

## Translation is not provenance

A translated dataset should retain original source language, target translation language, translation method, whether the source dialogue was originally produced in that culture, and whether the translation was human, machine, or hybrid.

A translated English corpus does not become an English-origin therapy corpus merely because its released text is English.

## Candidate workflow

Search → candidate record → evidence collection → metadata extraction → access/license verification → privacy/ethics review → human verification → verified registry record.

No automated discovery process should silently promote a candidate to verified status.

## Platforms to search

GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo, OSF, Dataverse, institutional repositories, DOI registries, university research portals, publisher supplementary materials, corpus repositories, and national research infrastructures.

## Research-language expansion

The initial search program should include at least English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Russian, Ukrainian, Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Hindi, Bengali, Urdu, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Indonesian, Malay, Vietnamese, Thai, Swahili, and other languages identified through discovery.

This is a search strategy, not a claim that therapy transcript resources exist for every language.

## Global coverage rule

The registry should actively search for evidence from every world region while preserving an explicit `unknown` state when geography, culture, language, licensing, or privacy information cannot be verified.

## Safety boundary

Do not scrape or redistribute restricted therapy transcripts. Store metadata, evidence, official URLs, access instructions, and provenance only.
