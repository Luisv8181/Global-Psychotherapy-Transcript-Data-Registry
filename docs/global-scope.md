# Global Scope: Multicontextual, Multilingual Psychotherapy Data

## Vision

The Global Psychotherapy Transcript Data Registry is intentionally **global, multilingual, and multicontextual**.

The registry should not treat psychotherapy data from the United States as the default representation of psychotherapy worldwide. It should map the research landscape wherever psychotherapy, counseling, psychological support, or related therapeutic dialogue has been documented and made available for legitimate research.

This means the registry indexes differences in:

- Country and region
- Language and dialect
- Cultural context
- Therapy modality
- Clinical setting
- Population and age group
- Mental-health system
- Longitudinal structure
- Data source and collection method
- Access and licensing constraints
- Privacy and de-identification practices

The goal is not to force global therapy data into one standardized cultural model. The goal is to make differences visible, searchable, and scientifically useful.

## Core principle

> **Global coverage is not translation coverage.**

A transcript translated into English is not equivalent to a therapy corpus originally produced in another linguistic and cultural context.

The registry therefore records both the language of the source interaction and any translation or localization applied to the released resource.

## Language and geography

Every dataset should be searchable by country, region, source language, dialect, cultural context, collection context, and population context.

Country must never be inferred solely from language. Spanish-language therapy data, for example, should not automatically be classified as Spain, Latin America, or the United States. The record should identify documented geographic context or mark it unknown.

Language metadata should distinguish original interaction language, transcript language, translation language, parallel or multilingual versions, dialect or regional variety, and code-switching when documented.

## Global discovery scope

The registry should actively seek resources from North America, Latin America and the Caribbean, Europe, Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia, Central Asia, Oceania, Indigenous communities, and diasporic communities.

This is a discovery scope, not a claim that every region currently has accessible transcript data.

## Source taxonomy

Records should distinguish real therapy/counseling interactions, clinician role-play, human simulation, synthetic AI dialogue, public therapy media, clinical Q&A, and therapy-adjacent mental-health dialogue. These categories must not be collapsed into one generic therapy-transcript category.

## Discovery strategy

The discovery pipeline should search institutional repositories, universities, GitHub, Hugging Face, Zenodo, OSF, Dataverse, DOI repositories, publishers, clinical research publications, corpus-linguistics repositories, national research infrastructure, and conference papers or supplementary datasets.

The registry stores metadata, evidence, access instructions, and official links. It does not redistribute restricted therapy transcripts.

## Global coverage metrics

Future dashboards should report coverage by country, region, source language, dialect, therapy modality, population, age group, clinical topic, longitudinal availability, access level, and privacy-evaluation status.

A simple dataset count is not enough. Coverage should be measured across multiple dimensions.

## Research gaps

The registry should expose underrepresented combinations such as:

country × language × culture × modality × population × longitudinal structure × access

This turns the registry into a research-gap instrument rather than a passive bibliography.

## Ethical boundary

“Global” does not mean “scrape everything.” Metadata and authoritative links are appropriate; copyrighted, restricted, confidential, or unauthorized therapy transcripts are not to be redistributed.

Unknown is a valid value. Missing geographic, cultural, language, consent, licensing, or privacy information must not be silently inferred.

## Long-term question

> What psychotherapy dialogue data exists worldwide, in what contexts, under what access conditions, and with what limitations?

And eventually:

> Where are the world's biggest blind spots in psychotherapy research data?
